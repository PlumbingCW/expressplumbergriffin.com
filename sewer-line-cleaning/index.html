If every fixture in the house is slow or backing up at the same time, the problem is almost always in the main sewer line. Stop running water, keep people away from any sewage on the floor, and call our emergency line, which is open 24/7. We schedule a licensed plumber and ask where your cleanout is so we can plan the fastest access.

Sewer line cleaning is the removal of a blockage from the main line that carries wastewater out of the house. A machine with a cutting head or a water jet clears the pipe through the cleanout, and a camera pass afterward confirms that nothing is left behind.

When a Main Line Backup Is an Emergency: Signs to Watch

A single slow sink is usually a local trap issue. The main line is the suspect when the pattern spreads across the house.

  • The toilet gurgles when the washing machine drains.
  • Water from a shower rises in the tub of another bathroom.
  • The lowest drain in the house, often a basement or garage floor drain, overflows first.
  • Several fixtures slow down on the same day.
  • You smell sewer gas near the cleanout or in the yard.

Each of those means the line downstream of the branches is restricted. Cleaning a single fixture will not help, so skip the plunger and call.

Finding and Using the Cleanout

A cleanout is a capped pipe, usually four inches wide, near the foundation, in the yard or in the crawlspace. It gives a machine straight access to the main line without pulling a toilet. If you do not know where yours is, tell us the age and style of the house and we help you find it.

Many older Griffin homes have no outside cleanout. In that case we work through a toilet or a roof vent, which takes longer and costs more, and we may recommend installing a cleanout so future cleaning is quick.

What Usually Blocks a Main Line

Roots, grease, wipes and paper account for most blockages. Roots enter at joints in clay or cast iron pipe and grow into a mat that catches everything else. Wipes sold as flushable do not break down like paper. Grease from the kitchen hardens farther down the line than you would expect.

If the cable stops at the same distance every time, a cleaning will not last, and sewer line repair is the real fix. When buildup keeps returning, hydro jetting cleans the wall far better than a cable.

Why We Camera-Check After Cleaning

A line that drains again is not always a line that is healthy. The camera shows whether the pipe is clean, whether roots remain and whether the pipe is cracked. That is the difference between a cleaning that lasts a year and a backup that returns in a week. Our sewer inspection service explains what the video can show.

Preventing the Next Backup

Keep wipes, feminine products and paper towels out of toilets, and keep cooking grease out of sinks. Plant trees away from the sewer line and have the line scoped if you live in an older house with mature trees. Homes in older Griffin neighborhoods, like those near downtown, benefit from a camera look every few years, since original pipe ages whether or not you notice.

Code Notes for Main Line Cleaning

Cleanouts must stay accessible under the plumbing code, which is one reason we ask where yours is before we arrive. Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

Georgia 811 marks buried utility lines before any digging, and a licensed plumber arranges the call for excavation work.
