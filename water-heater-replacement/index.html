Close the cold water valve on top of the tank, switch off the breaker or gas control, and call our 24/7 line so we can schedule a replacement as early as the day allows.

Water heater replacement is the removal of the failed unit and the installation of a new one, including connections, venting, relief valve and a test run. Planned swaps, new construction and fuel changes are handled on our water heater installation page.

When a Failed Tank Is an Emergency: What Replacement Looks Like

A typical emergency swap runs in order. The plumber confirms the failure and shows you the cause, explains the replacement options and price, drains and removes the old tank, sets and connects the new unit, checks venting or electrical supply, and runs hot water through every fixture to look for leaks.

Ask about tank size before the plumber arrives if you can. A household that has outgrown a 40 gallon tank may want a 50, and the choice affects floor space, venting and the price.

Choosing Between Tank, Tankless and Hybrid

Most emergency replacements go to the same type of tank that failed, because it fits the existing venting, gas line and electrical supply. That keeps the job to one visit and a predictable price.

A tankless unit gives continuous hot water and saves floor space, but it often needs a larger gas line, new venting and sometimes an electrical change, so it works better as a planned project. A heat pump hybrid is the most efficient option and needs space, airflow and a nearby drain. Our tankless water heater page walks through what each change involves.

Like-for-like tank swap

  • Same fuel, same location, usually one visit.
  • Lowest upfront cost.
  • Best choice when the failure is an emergency.
  • Works with the existing venting and shutoffs.

Upgrade to tankless or hybrid

  • Better efficiency and, for tankless, endless hot water.
  • May need new gas line, venting or electrical work.
  • Often scheduled after the emergency is handled.
  • Worth pricing when the old unit was due for replacement anyway.

What a Water Heater Failure Looks Like

Pooling water at the base, a tank that rusts at the seams, rust-colored hot water or a rumble that grows louder each month are the common signs. A tank over ten years old that leaks is replaced, not patched.

Griffin homes with the heater in a garage see the damage first as water creeping toward the slab edge, and homes with the heater in a closet or attic can have water reach drywall before anyone notices. Check the area around your heater monthly, and call quickly if you find moisture.

Permits, Inspection and Doing It Right

Water heater replacement is regulated work. The City of Griffin states that trade permits for plumbing, electrical and HVAC work must be pulled by licensed contractors, and the Permits Division at 100 S. Hill St. can answer questions at (770) 233-4130. Properties in unincorporated Spalding County work with Spalding County Community Development at (770) 467-4280.

Permit rules vary by the work and the property, so ask your local office. We tell you when a job needs one, and we recommend a gas or venting inspection when the change involves them.

What Happens to the Old Tank

The old tank is drained and removed as part of the job. Ask the plumber about it when you call so the scope is clear before work starts. If the old heater is leaking, take photos of the damage and the tank's data plate for your insurer.

Guidelines and Codes for a Replacement

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against.

For a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280. You can verify a plumber's Georgia license at verify.sos.ga.gov, the Georgia Secretary of State's verification site.
