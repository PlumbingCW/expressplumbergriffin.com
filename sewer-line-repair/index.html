Sewage backing up into the house is an emergency. Stop using water, keep everyone out of the affected area and call our 24/7 line, and we schedule a licensed plumber to find out whether the blockage is in your line or beyond it. Do not flush or run the washing machine.

Sewer line repair means fixing the pipe that carries wastewater from the house to the city sewer or a septic system. The repair may be a short spot repair, a pipe liner or a full replacement, and the choice depends on what a camera shows.

Sewer Backup: What to Do First

A backup is serious because wastewater carries bacteria. Keep people and pets away, avoid touching sewage with bare hands and open windows if the smell is strong. Do not use chemical drain cleaners on a sewage backup.

If you rent, tell your landlord and take photos. If you own, photograph the damage and call your insurer, since sewer backup coverage is typically an endorsement. We document what the camera finds, which helps with claims.

Finding the Break with a Camera

We insert a camera through a cleanout and record the line. That shows cracks, offset joints, sags that hold water, roots entering at joints and collapsed sections, and it gives the distance from the cleanout so the repair is dug in the right place. See sewer inspection for the camera service on its own.

A camera also tells us when the answer is not a repair. A line that is simply clogged needs cleaning, so we may start with sewer line cleaning and recheck.

Spot Repair, Lining or Replacement

A localized crack or root intrusion is a candidate for a spot repair, where we dig a small section and replace it. A longer run of aging pipe may suit trenchless lining, which creates a new pipe inside the old one without a long trench. A collapsed or badly sagging line usually needs replacement.

Older Pipe in Griffin Homes

Mill-era and downtown homes may still have clay or cast iron service lines. Clay joints let roots in and cast iron corrodes. Newer subdivisions use PVC, but poorly compacted trenches can cause sags. A camera tells you which story your line has.

Experiment homes may be on septic, which has its own rules. Our Experiment plumbing page explains how we handle septic questions.

Permits and Who Does What

The City of Griffin says sewer tie-ins and repairs need a trade permit from a licensed contractor. Call the Permits Division at (770) 233-4130 for your address. In unincorporated Spalding County, call Community Development at (770) 467-4280.

We explain what we will dig, where the permit applies and what the inspector checks, before you approve the work.

Codes and Permits for Sewer Work

Sewer repairs and tie-ins are among the most regulated plumbing work. Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

For a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280. Georgia 811 marks buried utility lines before any digging, and a licensed plumber arranges the call for excavation work.
