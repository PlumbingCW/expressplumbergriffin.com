Plumber in South Hill Street and College-Maple, Griffin, GA

South and southwest of downtown, the streets around South Hill, College and Maple hold some of Griffin's oldest and largest houses. Call to talk through a plumbing question at your address.

☎ Call (770) 214-4545.

The blocks around South Hill Street, College Street and Maple Street are what Griffin's own planning documents call a traditional neighborhood. They were built mostly before World War II, with sidewalks, street trees and a mix of single-family and multi-family housing.

If you own or rent a home here and have a drain, water heater, leak or sewer question, call (770) 214-4545. We ask about the age and layout of the house first, because a large older home often has more fixtures, longer pipe runs and more past repairs than a newer one.

What Griffin's Plans Say About This Area

The City of Griffin's 2018 to 2038 plan update labels the area around South Hill, College and Maple Streets a Traditional Neighborhood. It describes older residential areas developed before World War II, with pedestrian-friendly streets, sidewalks and street trees.

An earlier preservation plan from the University of Georgia describes a turn of the century area south and southwest of the central business district. It names Poplar, Meriwether, Hammond, Maple and College Streets and describes large high-style houses, many in brick with generous setbacks, plus a College-Maple district of early twentieth century architecture.

Large Older Houses Raise Different Questions

A house from the early 1900s may have been renovated several times. Bathrooms get added, kitchens move and attics become rooms. Each change adds branches to the plumbing, and not every branch is documented.

Age alone does not tell us what is inside the walls. A 1910 house may have been repiped, partly updated or left alone, and the only way to know is to look. That is why we start with questions and a camera or pressure check rather than a guess.

Street Trees, Sewer Lines and Slow Drains

Sidewalks and street trees are part of the area's charm, and the city plan lists them as features of a traditional neighborhood. Roots can find joints in any older sewer lateral, but the presence of a tree does not mean a line has failed.

If a main drain is slow or backs up more than once, a drain cleaning visit with a camera view shows whether the cause is grease, paper, roots or a broken section. We tell you what the picture shows before recommending repair.

Historic Charm and Permits: Ask Before You Start

Griffin's local historic district covers downtown only, and the city's own plan recommends designating residential historic districts. We have not found a designation that covers these blocks, but rules can change. Before exterior changes, ask the City of Griffin Planning and Development office at (770) 233-4130.

For plumbing permits inside the city, the Permits Division at 100 S. Hill St. handles them, and trade permits must be pulled by licensed contractors. A water heater replacement or sewer repair may need one, so confirm before work begins. Plumbing work in Georgia follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments published by the Department of Community Affairs, so the inspector applies the same code on every street.
