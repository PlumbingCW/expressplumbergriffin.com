Toilet repair is the diagnosis and fix of the part that has failed: a flapper, fill valve, wax ring, flush handle, supply line or the drain beyond the bowl. Toilet installation is removing an old toilet and setting a new one on a fresh seal with a level, secure fit.

If a toilet is overflowing right now, our emergency line is open 24/7. Shut the valve behind the bowl, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed, then call. If sewage is coming up in other fixtures too, see emergency drain cleaning, because the problem is deeper in the line.

When a Toilet Problem Is an Emergency

Treat it as urgent when a toilet overflows with sewage, when water pours from the tank or supply line, when the bowl keeps rising after a flush or when more than one fixture backs up. Those are signs of a broken part or a blocked line, and waiting spreads water into flooring and walls.

We schedule the first available licensed plumber, and after-hours calls follow the same process. Same-day repairs are offered when we have the part and the schedule allows.

Running Toilets and Wasted Water

A toilet that runs on its own is losing water through a worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve or a bad flush valve seat. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait fifteen minutes. If color appears in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.

A running toilet can add to your water bill every month without anyone noticing. A quick repair costs less than the water you lose, and it is worth fixing early.

Leaks at the Base or the Tank

Water at the base of the toilet usually points to a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts. Water at the tank bolts or supply line points to worn gaskets or a loose connection. Do not ignore a leak at the base, because water can rot the subfloor and stain the ceiling below.

If a floor feels soft or a ceiling stain appears below a bathroom, call quickly and mention it. Hidden damage like that may call for leak detection to find the source.

Clogs and Repeated Backups

A single clog is a plunger job. A toilet that clogs every few days, bubbles when another fixture drains or flushes weakly has a deeper issue, such as a partial blockage in the trap, a vent problem or a branch line that needs cleaning. We auger the toilet or pull it to inspect the trap, and we tell you what we find.

When to Replace Instead of Repair

Replace a toilet that cracks in the bowl or tank, rocks and leaks despite a new seal, or flushes poorly after repairs. Older toilets can also use far more water per flush than modern models.

Repair the toilet

  • Running, weak flush or small leaks.
  • Parts are cheap and quick.
  • Toilet bowl and tank are sound.
  • Best when the toilet is otherwise fine.

Replace the toilet

  • Cracks, repeated leaks or constant clogs.
  • Old high-volume models.
  • Remodels and height or comfort upgrades.
  • Best when repair cost approaches new.

Installing a New Toilet

Installation includes shutting off and disconnecting the old unit, checking the flange, setting a new seal, leveling the bowl, connecting the supply and testing flushes. A damaged flange is the surprise we see most often, and we repair or replace it so the toilet sits solid.

If the bathroom floor is uneven or the flange sits low or high, tell us. We handle it so the toilet does not rock after you leave.

What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Lift the tank lid and hold the flapper closed to stop more water entering.
  • Lay towels around the base and wear gloves for any sewage contact.
  • Keep children and pets out of the bathroom until the area is cleaned.
  • Skip chemical drain openers, because they sit in the bowl and splash back.

Standards for Toilet Work

Toilet height, flange position and water use per flush are covered by plumbing code and fixture standards. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against.

For a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280.
