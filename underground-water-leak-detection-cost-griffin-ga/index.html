Underground Water Leak Detection Cost: Griffin, GA Price Guide

☎ Call (770) 214-4545.
By the Griffin Express Plumbing team.Published 2026.Griffin, GA.

An underground water leak hides where you cannot see it, and the first sign is often a bill that jumped. The cost of finding it depends on the method, the size of the property and how hard the leak is to reach. This guide explains what you are paying for, so you can compare quotes without guessing at a number.

We do not publish a flat price, because the factors vary house to house. We do tell you what each factor does. If you suspect a leak now, our leak detection service starts with a meter test that costs little time and tells us whether to go further.

What You Pay For

Detection is a diagnostic service. You are paying for a plumber's time, the equipment used and a written finding that says where the leak is and how bad it is. Repair is a separate scope, and it should be quoted separately.

Methods in Plain Language

A meter test checks whether water moves through the meter when every fixture is off. A pressure test isolates a section of line and watches for a drop. Acoustic listening uses sensitive microphones to hear water escaping from a pipe. Thermal imaging shows a warm line under a floor. Tracer gas fills a line with a harmless gas that rises at the leak.

A good plumber starts with the cheapest, fastest test and escalates only if needed. You should hear why each test is being used.

Yard Lines and Service Lines

A leak between the meter and the house is a service line leak, and it is the homeowner's to repair. Signs include a soggy patch in the yard, lush green grass over the line or a drop in water pressure. If the leak is in the yard, the next step is often a water line repair with a dig at the marked spot.

Before any digging, utility lines must be marked. Georgia law requires a call to 811 before excavation, and a plumber arranges it.

How to Compare Quotes

Ask what is included, whether the written report is part of the price, whether the quote covers locating only or locating and opening the ground, and whether a repair price is given separately. Be wary of any quote that offers to dig before the leak is located. Finding the leak first saves digging in the wrong place.

  • Is the meter test included?
  • Which locating method will you use?
  • Do I get a written finding and photos?
  • Is excavation or restoration part of this price?
  • Will the repair quote be separate?

Insurance and Documentation

Some homeowners policies pay for resulting water damage from a sudden leak, and some pay part of the cost to access and find the source. Many do not pay to repair the pipe itself. Call your insurer before work starts and ask what they need from a plumber's report.

Why a Cheap Dig Can Cost More

The most expensive way to find a leak is to dig where you guess it might be. A trench in the wrong place costs time, damages landscaping and leaves the leak running. Locating before digging narrows the excavation to a small area, and that is where the savings come from.

Ask any contractor who offers to start with a backhoe how they know where to dig. A good answer names a test, such as a pressure test or acoustic listening. A vague answer is a reason to get a second opinion.

What the Written Report Should Contain

A useful report states the meter reading before and after, the test methods used, the approximate location and depth of the leak, the likely cause and the recommended repair. Photos and a simple sketch help. Insurers and utilities often want this document, and so do buyers if you sell.

Keep the report with your home records. If another leak appears later, you have a baseline for comparison, and our leak detection team can use it.

Leak Types That Change the Price

Not every leak costs the same to find. A supply line leak holds pressure and makes noise, which makes it easier to hear. A drain leak is a different search, and it may need a camera or a dye test. A leak at a fitting is quick to confirm, while a pinhole in a long run may need several tests.

What to Do While You Wait for the Plumber

  • Close the main water valve if water is spreading.
  • Read the meter and write down the number and time.
  • Photograph wet spots, stains and the meter for your insurer.
  • Keep power away from standing water and call an electrician if outlets are wet.
  • Avoid digging anywhere until Georgia 811 has marked utilities.

Sources for Utility and Insurance Questions

For billing adjustments, ask City of Griffin customer service what its guidelines allow. For coverage, ask your insurer for its written standards on water damage and access costs. Georgia 811 marks buried utility lines before any digging, and a licensed plumber arranges the call for excavation work.

Water Bills, Meters and the City of Griffin

If you are on city water, the meter sits at the property line and the pipe from there to the house is yours to maintain. Call City of Griffin customer service to learn where the dividing point is on your lot and ask what the billing office offers if a leak caused a spike. Experiment homes may have a different water provider, so ask your provider.
