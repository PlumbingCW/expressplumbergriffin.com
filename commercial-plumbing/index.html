Commercial plumbing is service on the plumbing systems of non-residential buildings, including shops, restaurants, offices, churches, warehouses and apartment properties. Fixtures, pipe sizes, water demand and code requirements are different from a house, and downtime has a cost.

Emergencies That Stop a Business

The calls we hear most are backed-up restroom lines, kitchen grease blockages, burst supply lines, water heater failures and floor drains that overflow. Each one affects whether a business can stay open.

  • Shut the main water valve if a pipe is flooding the space.
  • Stop using restrooms and sinks if drains are backing up.
  • Protect inventory and electronics from standing water.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer before cleanup.
  • Call us and tell us whether the building is occupied.

We schedule the first available licensed plumber, and after-hours calls follow the same process.

Restaurants, Kitchens and Grease Lines

Food service lines carry grease, starch and food solids, and they clog on a schedule set by your menu and volume. A grease trap or interceptor needs regular service, and the lines beyond it need periodic cleaning. Hydro jetting is the standard tool for keeping a kitchen line open between pump-outs.

Floor drains, mop sinks and hand sinks follow health department rules for your type of operation. Ask your local health office about what your inspection requires, and call us to bring the plumbing up to that standard.

Retail, Offices and Shopping Centers

Along the North Expressway corridor and in centers like North Griffin Square, long horizontal drain lines carry restrooms for many tenants. A backup in one unit can come from the shared line, so we trace the problem before we clear it. A camera inspection tells us whether the line is a cleaning job or a repair, and our sewer inspection page explains the process.

Apartments and Multi-Unit Properties

Property managers need fast answers and clear records. We document what we find, tell you which unit or shared line is affected and note repeat issues so you can plan repairs. Aging stacks, shared water heaters and corroded supply lines are the usual capital items, and we price them before work starts. For older buildings, repiping is the long-term answer to repeated leaks.

Scheduled Maintenance

Preventive service costs less than emergency calls. Water heater flushes, drain cleaning, backflow checks and fixture repairs can be scheduled during hours that suit your operation. Call to talk through the building and we recommend a schedule.

Code and Health Guidelines for Businesses

Commercial buildings answer to the plumbing code and to local health and fire rules that vary by business type. Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

Your local health office sets food service requirements, and for a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280.
