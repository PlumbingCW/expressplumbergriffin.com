Under-Counter Reverse Osmosis Filtration for Griffin, GA Homes

☎ Call (770) 214-4545.
By the Griffin Express Plumbing team.Published 2026.Griffin, GA.

A reverse osmosis system pushes water through a very fine membrane that blocks many dissolved substances. Under-counter units treat the water for one tap, usually a small faucet at the kitchen sink for drinking and cooking. They are a popular add-on, and the install is a plumbing job with a few details that matter.

This guide explains how an RO unit works, what the installation needs under the sink and what to ask before you buy. For the fixture side of the work, see faucet repair, which covers sink fixtures we also install.

How Reverse Osmosis Works

Household pressure forces water through a semipermeable membrane. Clean water collects in a small storage tank, and the rejected water carries away what the membrane held back and goes to the drain. Most units add a sediment prefilter, a carbon prefilter and a carbon post filter for taste.

The membrane is rated for how well it reduces dissolved solids, and certifications list what a specific model is tested to reduce. Read the certification, not the marketing.

The unit sits in the cabinet under the sink, and several connections make it work.

Water Use and the Reject Stream

RO units send some water to the drain for every gallon of treated water they make. The ratio varies by model and by your water pressure. Ask for the manufacturer's rating and think about whether the added drain use matters to you.

Low household pressure slows production and wastes more water. If your pressure is weak, check the cause first. Our water line repair page explains pressure problems that start outside the house.

Upkeep Schedule

Prefilters need regular replacement, often every six to twelve months. The membrane lasts longer, often a few years, depending on water quality. Sanitizing the system and checking the tank air pressure are part of good maintenance.

  • Replace sediment and carbon prefilters on schedule.
  • Replace the membrane when output or taste declines.
  • Replace the post filter with the prefilters.
  • Check for leaks under the sink at every change.
  • Mark the dates on the cabinet wall.

Is RO Right for You

RO is a good fit when you want treated drinking water at one tap and your water report suggests a reason. It is not needed for every household, and it does not treat the water at the shower or the washing machine. Start from your water report and your goals.

Space, Faucets and Counter Holes

Under-sink space is tight, and RO units need room for a tank, filters and tubing. Check your cabinet before you buy. A deep sink, a disposal and a trash pull-out can crowd the space. Some units mount on a wall inside the cabinet or in an adjacent cabinet.

The RO faucet needs a hole in the sink or counter. Many sinks have a spare hole from a soap dispenser or sprayer. If not, drilling stone or stainless takes care. A plumber measures first and plans the best spot.

Water Pressure and Tank Performance

RO systems work best with steady household pressure. Low pressure slows production and wastes more water to the drain. The storage tank has an air bladder that must be charged to the right pressure, and a flat bladder makes the tap sputter. A plumber can check both.

If your whole house has weak pressure, find the cause first. See water line repair and our service areas for what we cover.

Taste, Minerals and Remineralizing

RO water has very low mineral content, and some people find it tastes flat. Some systems add a remineralizing stage that returns small amounts of minerals for taste. This is a preference. If you notice a taste change after your system is installed, ask the plumber about the post filter or a remineralizing cartridge.

Do not use RO water for aquariums or other special uses without checking with the equipment maker, since mineral needs differ.

Standards for Drain Connections and Certification

The drain connection for an RO unit follows plumbing code on air gaps and trap placement, and the unit's certification sheet names the standard it was tested to.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

Know Your Water First

Ask the City of Griffin or your provider for its annual water quality report before choosing a treatment system. We do not cite a Griffin water figure here because we have not verified one for your address. For more detail, a lab test of a tap sample shows exactly what your water holds.
