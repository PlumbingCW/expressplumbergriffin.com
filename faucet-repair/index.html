If a faucet is spraying or will not shut off, close the shutoff valves under the sink or the main valve and call our emergency line, which is open 24/7. Faucet repair means finding the worn part behind a drip, leak or stiff handle and replacing it, or replacing the faucet when it is past saving.

Most faucet problems come from a small set of parts: cartridges, O-rings, washers, seats and aerators. Knowing which kind of faucet you have tells us which part is likely to be at fault, so tell us the brand if you can read it.

When a Faucet Problem Is an Emergency

A faucet that will not shut off, a handle that has come off under pressure or a supply line spraying inside the cabinet needs a plumber now. Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main valve if the stop will not turn, and call.

We schedule the first available licensed plumber at any hour. Same-day repairs are offered when we have the part and the schedule allows, and a stuck shutoff valve is replaced as part of the job.

Why a Faucet Drips

A dripping spout means water is slipping past a worn seal inside the valve. Compression faucets with two handles usually need new washers and seats. Cartridge and ball style faucets need a new cartridge or a rebuild kit. Ceramic disc faucets need new discs or a cartridge.

A drip of one drop per second can waste thousands of gallons a year, so a repair pays for itself.

Leaks at the Base, Handle or Supply Line

Water around the base of the spout points to worn O-rings. Water at the handle points to a loose or worn packing. Water under the sink may come from a supply line, a shutoff valve or the drain, so check where the water starts before blaming the faucet.

Wet cabinet floors often rot out slowly. If you find swollen wood or a musty smell under a sink, mention it when you call. A hidden leak behind a wall is a leak detection job.

Low Pressure and Clogged Aerators

Griffin water carries minerals that build up in aerators and cartridges. A faucet with weak flow may only need a cleaned aerator. If the whole house has low pressure, the cause may be elsewhere, such as a failing shutoff, corroded pipe or a leak. See our water line repair page for pressure problems that start outside the house.

Repair or Replace the Faucet

Replace when parts are no longer made, when the finish has worn through, when the faucet is corroded or when you want a new style. We install kitchen faucets with pull-down sprayers, bath faucets, tub and shower valves and outdoor hose bibs.

Outdoor Faucets and Freeze Season

Outdoor spigots can split during a hard freeze even in Georgia, and a split pipe inside the wall leaks only when the faucet is on. If you see water inside the house after using the hose, shut the valve to that line and call us. Frost-proof sillcocks last longer and are worth the upgrade.

Standards for Fixtures and Supply Lines

Faucets and supply lines are built to fixture standards, and replacements should match the rating on the box. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against. Larger changes, such as moving a sink, follow the plumbing code.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.
