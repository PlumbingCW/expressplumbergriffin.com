Garbage Disposal Flange Installation: A Griffin, GA Guide

☎ Call (770) 214-4545.
By the Griffin Express Plumbing team.Published 2026.Griffin, GA.

The sink flange is the ring that holds a garbage disposal to the bottom of the kitchen sink and seals the opening. When it is installed well, you never think about it. When it is not, water seeps into the cabinet below, and the leak can run for months before anyone notices.

This guide explains how the flange, plumber's putty and mounting assembly fit together, so you can judge whether to tackle the job or call a plumber. If you already see a leak, our garbage disposal repair page explains the fix.

What the Flange Does

The flange sits in the sink drain hole. A rubber gasket, a fiber gasket, a mounting ring and a snap ring clamp it from below, and plumber's putty or a gasket seals the upper rim. The disposal then hangs from the mounting assembly and drains into the trap.

  • Sink flange: the visible ring in the drain opening.
  • Gaskets: seal the flange against the sink from below.
  • Mounting ring and snap ring: clamp the assembly together.
  • Disposal body: hangs from the mounting assembly.
  • Discharge tube and trap: carry water onward.

A weak seal at any layer lets water run down the outside of the unit.

Installing the Flange Step by Step

The sequence matters more than the tools.

Prep the sink

Remove the old flange and scrape off old putty. Clean the sink around the opening.

Seal the flange

Roll a rope of plumber's putty and press it under the flange rim, then seat it in the drain.

Clamp from below

Slide on the gasket, mounting ring and snap ring, and tighten the screws evenly until the putty squeezes out.

Wipe and test

Trim the squeezed putty, let it set and run water to check for drips.

Where Flange Leaks Come From

Leaks appear at the putty line, at the snap ring or around the gasket. Old putty dries and cracks. Uneven tightening leaves a gap on one side. A flange set at an angle leaks at one edge.

A leak under the sink does not always start at the flange. The dishwasher inlet, the drain tailpiece and the trap are also suspects. Dry everything and run water one step at a time to find where the drip begins.

Do It Yourself or Call a Plumber

A handy homeowner can replace a flange with basic tools. It becomes a plumber's job when the sink is cracked around the opening, the disposal is stuck to the old flange, the drain line is also clogged or the cabinet floor is already damp or soft.

If a disposal is being replaced anyway, a plumber can also reconnect the dishwasher drain and check the trap. See faucet repair if the sink fixture needs work at the same time.

Testing Before You Close the Cabinet

Run water for several minutes with a paper towel under each joint. Run the disposal briefly and check again. Look at the cabinet floor after a few hours. A dry towel after a full test means the seal holds.

Tools and Materials You Will Want

A flange install does not need exotic tools, but missing one slows the job. Gather everything before you start so the putty does not skin over while you hunt for a screwdriver.

  • Plumber's putty or the sealant the manufacturer specifies.
  • A flat screwdriver for the snap ring and mounting screws.
  • A putty knife for cleaning the old seal.
  • Channel-lock pliers for the slip nuts.
  • A bucket and towels for drips from the trap.
  • A flashlight, because the cabinet is dark.

If your disposal came with a kit, use the gaskets and rings in the box rather than mixing parts from an older unit.

Sink Materials Change the Approach

Stainless steel sinks are thin, and an overtightened mounting ring can dimple them. Porcelain and cast iron sinks are brittle around the drain and can chip. Granite composite and solid-surface sinks may call for a specific sealant. Read the instruction sheet for your sink, and when in doubt call the manufacturer.

If the opening in the sink is rough, rusted or cracked, the flange will never seal, and the right fix is a different sink or a plumber's judgment.

The Dishwasher Connection

Many disposals have a small inlet for the dishwasher drain hose. A new disposal often has a plug in that inlet that must be knocked out before the hose is attached, and forgetting it makes the dishwasher back up into the sink. If your dishwasher drains poorly after a disposal install, check that knockout plug first.

Also check the high loop in the dishwasher hose. Without it, dirty water can flow back into the dishwasher. If you are not sure, our garbage disposal repair team can look at the whole sink base, and the Experiment page explains how we reach homes north of Griffin.

Manufacturer Guidelines for the Flange

The disposal maker's guidelines decide the sealant and the order of parts, and the plumbing code decides the trap and drain arrangement. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against.

Kitchen Plumbing in Older Griffin Homes

Griffin has many older homes where sinks, cabinets and drain lines have been modified over decades. An old cast iron or galvanized tailpiece may be corroded, and replacing the flange can expose a weak fitting. Tell the plumber the age of the house, and they look at the whole sink base, not only the disposal.
