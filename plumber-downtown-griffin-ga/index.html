Plumber in Downtown Griffin, GA

Downtown Griffin is a historic commercial district with restaurants, shops and offices, and housing above some storefronts. Call to talk through a plumbing question for your building.

Downtown Griffin is the city's historic commercial core, centered on Hill Street and Taylor Street. This page is for businesses, landlords and tenants downtown, and it is not a residential subdivision page. The National Register listing dates the district to 1894, and plumbing in an older mixed-use building raises questions that a single house does not.

If you manage a restaurant, a retail space, an office or an apartment above a storefront, call (770) 214-4545 and tell us what is happening. We will ask about the building, the fixtures involved and who is responsible for the line.

What Makes Downtown Griffin Different

The Griffin Commercial Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on November 16, 1988. The listing describes the district as roughly bounded by Central Alley, Sixth Street, Taylor Street and Eighth Street, and notes buildings along North Hill Street and East Solomon Street.

The city also created a local Historic Downtown Griffin Commercial District in 2007 under its 2002 Historic Preservation Ordinance. The city's 2018 to 2038 plan update encourages residential units above commercial storefronts, which is why apartments and lofts appear above some storefronts.

Exterior Changes May Need Review First

Inside the local historic district, owners must get a Certificate of Appropriateness before making changes to a property, including exterior work and signs. The Historic Preservation Commission reviews applications at public hearings on the second and fourth Wednesday of each month, and applications go through the Office of Planning and Zoning at One Griffin Center.

We cannot tell you what the commission will require. If a plumbing job could change something visible from outside, such as a vent, a rooftop unit, exposed piping or a wall opening, ask Planning and Development at (770) 233-4130 before ordering parts.

Plumbing work in Georgia follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with Georgia amendments published by the Department of Community Affairs. Permits for plumbing work inside the city go through the City of Griffin Permits Division, and trade permits must be pulled by licensed contractors.

Plumbing Questions That Come With Older Commercial Buildings

A restaurant, a retail shop and an apartment above a storefront all use the building differently. We keep our questions simple and practical.

Restaurant and bar drains

Kitchens and bars send grease, food waste and a lot of water down the line. Ask the city whether your use has grease requirements, and see hydro jetting for scouring a stubborn line.

Shared lines in mixed-use buildings

When an apartment sits above a shop, a backup in one unit can reach another. See commercial plumbing for what we look at.

Water heaters for small businesses

A shop, salon or kitchen can outgrow a residential tank. See water heater replacement for sizing questions.

Leaks inside old walls

Water behind plaster or brick may travel before it shows. Leak detection helps locate the source without opening more wall than needed.

First Steps When a Downtown Building Has a Leak or Backup

A few simple actions limit damage in any older building. They do not replace a plumber, but they make the first call more useful.

  1. Find the main shutoff for your space and close it if water is spraying or pooling.
  2. Move stock, files and electronics away from the wet area and photograph the damage.
  3. Check floor drains, restrooms and neighboring spaces to see how far the problem reaches.
  4. Tell the landlord or property manager, because shared lines and insurance may be involved.
  5. Call (770) 214-4545 and describe the address, the use of the space and what you found.

Landlords, Tenants and Who Is Responsible

In a leased space, the lease usually says who pays for plumbing repairs. Before you call anyone, check whether the landlord or property manager should be the one to hire a plumber.

If a blockage is in the main line that serves several spaces, the owner is usually the right contact. If it is inside your own space, the lease decides. Either way, tell us which fixtures are affected and whether neighbors see the same problem.
