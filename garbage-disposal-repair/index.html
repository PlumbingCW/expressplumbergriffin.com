A garbage disposal sits under the kitchen sink and grinds food scraps small enough to wash down the drain. When it jams, hums, leaks or floods the cabinet, call our emergency line, which is open 24/7. Turn off the disposal switch, close the shutoffs under the sink if water is leaking, and never put a hand inside the unit.

Disposal repair covers freeing a jam, resetting or replacing the motor, fixing leaks at the flange, hose or housing, and clearing the drain line the disposal feeds. If the sink is full of standing water, the clog may be past the disposal, so see drain cleaning.

When a Disposal Problem Is an Emergency

A disposal that leaks steadily into the cabinet, a sink that fills and will not drain or a unit that smokes or trips the breaker needs attention right away. Switch it off, close the water shutoffs under the sink if water is escaping and keep the circuit off if the unit smells burnt.

We schedule the first available licensed plumber at any hour, and same-day repairs are offered when we have the part and the schedule allows.

Humming but Not Spinning

A humming disposal that will not turn usually has a jam. A spoon, bone, fruit pit or fibrous food can wedge the grinding plate. With the power off, many disposals have a hex socket on the underside where a wrench turns the plate to free it, and the red reset button on the bottom restarts a tripped motor.

If the unit hums again after you free it, the motor may have burned out, and replacement is likely cheaper than repair on an older unit.

Leaks Under the Sink

A disposal can leak from the sink flange, the drain connection, the dishwasher inlet or the housing. A leak from the flange is a sealing issue we can fix. A leak from the body means the housing has cracked and the unit is replaced.

Wipe the disposal dry and run water for a minute with a paper towel underneath to see where the drip starts. That tells us what part is involved before we arrive.

Smells and Slow Draining

Odors come from food residue stuck in the grinding chamber and under the splash guard. Ice cubes and citrus peel help in the short term. A sink that drains slowly even with the disposal running often has a grease-lined drain, and the fix is cleaning the line, not the disposal.

What Not to Put Down a Disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in volume, pasta, rice, potato peels, celery and eggshells in bulk are the usual troublemakers. They swell, stick or cling inside the trap arm. Run cold water before, during and after use, and cut large scraps small.

A disposal is a convenience and not a trash can. Our drain crews see more kitchen clogs from heavy disposal use than from almost anything else.

Replacement and Horsepower

A standard disposal runs from one third to one horsepower. Heavier units grind better and run quieter, but they cost more. We match the unit to the household and the existing mounting.

If you also have a leaking faucet or a failing drain, we can handle it in the same visit. See faucet repair for sink fixture work.

Installation Guidelines for Disposals

Disposals are installed to the maker's guidelines, including the sink flange seal, the electrical connection and the drain trap. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.
