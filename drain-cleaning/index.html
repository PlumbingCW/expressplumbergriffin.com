A drain that has stopped completely, a toilet that backs up into the tub or water rising through a floor drain is an emergency, and our emergency drain cleaning line is open 24/7 for exactly that. While you wait for the plumber, stop running water anywhere in the house, keep children and pets away from backed-up sewage, and put the plunger down if more than one fixture is affected.

Drain cleaning means removing a blockage from a drain or sewer line with a cable, a camera-guided tool or a water jet, then confirming that water flows freely before the plumber leaves. Most Griffin clogs begin small: grease and food scraps in a kitchen line, hair and soap in a tub, wipes and paper in a toilet branch.

When a Clogged Drain Becomes an Emergency

Treat it as urgent when two or more fixtures back up at once, when the lowest drain in the house gurgles or overflows, or when you smell sewage. Those signs usually point to the main line rather than a single trap, and waiting lets the water find its way into flooring and cabinets.

  • Stop using sinks, tubs, dishwashers and washing machines so the line stops filling.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into a fully blocked line, because the liquid sits there and can burn whoever opens the pipe next.
  • Move rugs and stored boxes away from floor drains and the lowest bathroom.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and which still work. That tells the plumber where the blockage sits.

Call us after hours without worrying about the clock. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and an urgent backup goes to the front of the line.

Kitchen Sink Clogs

The kitchen line takes the hardest abuse in any house. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, coffee grounds and rice swell, and a garbage disposal pushes fine particles into a trap that was never meant to carry them. A kitchen line that drains slowly for weeks usually clears in one visit once the grease ring is cut out with a cable.

If the dishwasher drains into the same line, it will often back up into the sink at the same time. If the disposal hums but the sink is still full, the clog is past the disposal, and our garbage disposal repair page explains when the unit itself is the problem.

Cabling, Camera or Jet: Matching the Tool to the Clog

A hand or machine cable breaks through soft clogs such as hair, paper and grease, and it is the fastest fix for a single fixture. When a line clears and then clogs again within a few weeks, the cable only punched a hole, and the walls of the pipe are still caked.

That is when we step up to a camera to see the real cause and then to hydro jetting to scour the pipe wall. If the camera finds a crack, a sag or tree roots, the answer shifts to sewer line repair instead of another cleaning.

Shower, Tub and Toilet Branch Clogs

Hair and soap scum slow shower drains for months before they stop, and the fix is usually a quick cable or a pull of the hair mass. A toilet that clogs more than once a month and also bubbles in a nearby tub points to the branch line, not the bowl. For the bowl itself, see toilet repair and installation.

A drain line that leaks is a different job. If a ceiling below a bathroom stains or a cabinet floor stays damp even though the drain runs clear, call us and mention it, because a cracked or loose drain joint needs a repair and not a cleaning.

Older Griffin Homes, Roots and Aging Lines

Downtown Griffin and the mill-era streets have homes built long before PVC existed, and the original drains under or behind them may be cast iron or clay. Cast iron rusts and scales on the inside, which narrows the pipe, and clay joints invite tree roots looking for water.

Newer homes in the ZIP codes 30223 and 30224 more often have PVC branches, but builder-grade lines can still sag and hold water, which catches paper and grease. Either way, a camera view before a repair decision saves money, and our sewer inspection service documents what the line looks like.

Experiment sits just north of the city, and homes there on septic or older lines have their own habits, covered on our Experiment emergency plumbing page.

Codes and Guidelines Behind Drain Work

Drain, trap and vent layouts are judged against code, not preference. Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

A repair that changes slope, venting or a cleanout can trigger a permit, so confirm with the local office first. For a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280.
