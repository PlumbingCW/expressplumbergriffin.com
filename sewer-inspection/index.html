A camera on a cable shows what is inside your sewer line, so you decide with facts and not guesses.

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera through a cleanout or fixture opening and records the inside of the line. The video shows cracks, offset joints, roots, sags and scale, and it gives distances so a repair can be planned.

This is a planned service. If sewage is backing up now, start with emergency sewer line repair and we will camera-inspect as part of the fix.

When a Camera Inspection Makes Sense

Order an inspection before buying an older home, after repeated clogs, when a yard stays soggy over the sewer line, or before you pour concrete near the line. A home inspector may not scope the sewer, and a repair surprise after closing is expensive.

Many Griffin buyers are looking at older downtown houses or mill cottages where original lines remain. A camera view gives you something concrete to negotiate with and avoids surprises.

What the Camera Can and Cannot See

The camera sees pipe condition, roots, standing water, offsets and the transition to the main. It does not see leaks that only occur under pressure, and it cannot pass through a full blockage. If the pipe is clogged, we clear it first, as explained on sewer line cleaning.

We save a video and explain it in plain language. If a repair is needed, you get options and a clear price.

Locating the Line and Depth

With a locator, we can mark the line's path and depth at the surface. That helps when planning an addition, a fence or a pool. Knowing where the pipe runs prevents a shovel from finding it first.

Pre-Purchase and Landlord Use

Property managers use inspections to document the condition of lines across rental units and to plan cleaning schedules. Investors use them to price repairs before closing. See commercial plumbing for multi-unit work.

What Shows Up on the Video

The camera records the inside of the pipe as it travels, and the plumber narrates what the picture shows. Common findings are easy to name once you know what they look like.

What you seeWhat it meansUsual next step.
Fine roots at a jointRoots found a small gapCut back and monitor, or repair the joint.
A dip holding waterA bellied sectionClean, then plan a repair if it keeps catching debris.
Hairline crackPipe is stressedMonitor or spot repair.
Offset jointPipe sections have shiftedSpot repair.
Heavy scale or greaseWall buildupHydro jetting.
Collapsed sectionPipe has failedExcavation or lining.

Seeing the picture yourself is the best way to understand the problem. Ask for the recording, and keep it with your home records.

How We Prepare for a Pre-Purchase Inspection

If you are buying a house, call as soon as the contract allows an inspection period. Ask the seller to give access to the cleanouts and the lowest toilet. Tell us the age of the home and whether it is on city sewer or septic, since septic is a different system.

We run the camera, locate the line if you ask and explain what we found in plain terms. You decide whether to ask the seller for repairs or a credit. See sewer line repair if the video shows something that needs fixing, and our Experiment page if the house is north of Griffin.

Inspection After a Repair or Cleaning

A camera pass after a repair or a cleaning is proof that the work did its job. It shows that the new section sits at the right slope, that joints are tight and that no debris is left. Keep the video for your records. If you sell the house later, a recent inspection is a selling point, and it can shorten negotiations.

Standards Around Camera Inspections

A camera inspection is a diagnostic service, and it does not require a permit by itself, but any repair that follows is judged against code.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules.

For a permit question at a Griffin address, call the City of Griffin Permits Division at (770) 233-4130, and for unincorporated Spalding County call Community Development at (770) 467-4280.
