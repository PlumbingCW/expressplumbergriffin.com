If sewage is backing into a tub, a floor drain or a toilet and a cable has already failed, hydro jetting is often the next step. Our emergency line is open 24/7, and while you wait you should stop using water and keep everyone clear of the backed-up area.

Hydro jetting uses a hose with a rotating nozzle to blast water at high pressure against the inside of a drain or sewer line. It cuts grease, scale, sludge and small roots from the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the middle of the clog.

What Hydro Jetting Does That a Snake Cannot

A cable punches through soft material and leaves the buildup stuck to the wall. A jet scours the whole circumference, which is why it is the answer for lines that clog every few weeks. Commercial kitchens use jetting to keep grease from rebuilding.

When the first cable fails, ask the plumber for a camera pass first. The camera shows whether the problem is buildup, roots or a broken pipe, and the jet is only the right tool when the pipe is intact. Our sewer inspection page explains the camera step.

When a Backup Calls for Jetting Right Away

We recommend jetting for a main line blocked by heavy grease, for restaurant and shop lines, for lines with scale from age and for lines with root hairs that a cable cut but did not remove. If the camera shows a collapsed or cracked pipe, jetting can make things worse, and the right answer is sewer line repair.

Same-day jetting is offered when available, and after-hours calls get the same camera-first check.

What Jetting Cannot Do

Jetting does not repair a cracked pipe, bridge a bellied section or remove a solid obstruction such as a toy or a broken fitting. It also should not be used blindly on fragile older lines.

That is why we start with a camera when the line allows. If your home has older cast iron or clay, we explain the risk and recommend the right pressure and nozzle.

Hydro Jetting Versus Other Methods

Choosing the right tool is half the job. A cable is the fastest fix for a single soft clog. A camera tells you what is happening in the pipe. A jet cleans the wall. Using the wrong one wastes time or hurts the pipe.

What to Expect on the Day

The plumber finds access, usually a cleanout, runs a camera if the line allows and then threads the jetting hose in. Water pressure is raised gradually while the nozzle moves down the pipe. The plumber watches the flow and pulls back along the line to scour the wall a second time.

After jetting, a second camera pass confirms the line is clear. You see the result, and we explain what we found and whether the line needs follow-up. The work area is cleaned up, and any wastewater that came back at the cleanout is handled.

Standards That Apply to Jetting and Line Work

Jetting itself is a cleaning method, but anything that follows, such as a cleanout install or a pipe repair, is measured against the plumbing code.

Georgia's state minimum plumbing standard is built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), with Georgia amendments published by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, and local governments can add their own rules. Georgia 811 marks buried utility lines before any digging, and a licensed plumber arranges the call for excavation work.
