If water is actively coming through a ceiling, soaking a wall or pooling on a floor, shut off the main valve and call our emergency line, which is open 24/7. Turning off the water stops the damage while we schedule a licensed plumber to find the source.

Leak detection is the process of locating a hidden water leak using tools such as listening gear, pressure tests, thermal imaging and meter checks, then marking the exact spot so the repair opens as little as possible. Most leaks start with a symptom and not a drip, which is why diagnosis comes first.

When a Hidden Leak Is an Emergency: Symptoms to Watch

Many leaks are found because something looks or sounds wrong. These are the signs that bring most Griffin callers to us.

  • The meter dial or low-flow indicator moves when every fixture is off.
  • The water bill rises with no change in how the household uses water.
  • A warm spot on a slab floor or the sound of running water in a quiet house.
  • Stains, bubbling paint or musty smells on a ceiling or wall.
  • Water pressure that drops without explanation.

If you can, write down the meter reading, wait an hour with all water off and read it again. That simple test tells you whether a leak exists, and we use it to decide where to start.

The Meter Test and High Water Bills

The water meter is the first detective. If the meter keeps moving while the house is quiet, water is going somewhere between the meter and the fixtures. Before you assume a pipe has burst, check toilets for silent running and irrigation lines for breaks.

Griffin customers on city water should also remember that the water utility may have its own leak adjustment rules. Call the City of Griffin customer service about billing questions, and call us to find the leak. The next step on your side of the meter might be a water line repair if the break is underground.

Ceiling, Wall and Attic Leaks

A stained ceiling is not always above the leak. Water follows framing and pipe runs before it appears, so we trace it back with moisture meters and thermal imaging. Many ceiling leaks come from supply lines, drain joints, a failing shower pan or a condensate line from the HVAC system.

Show us the stain and tell us what is above it. The more we know about the floor plan, the faster we find the source.

Slab, Under-House and Buried Line Leaks

Griffin has many homes on concrete slabs. A leak in a line under a slab can warm the floor, stain flooring or raise the bill without any water showing. Acoustic listening and pressure tests locate it, and the slab is opened only at the marked spot. Our slab leak repair page explains the repair choices.

Homes with crawlspaces can have leaks that pool in the dark. We check the crawlspace and look for moisture, mold odor and rusted supply lines. Buried leaks in a yard show up as wet soil or a patch of grass that stays green and soft.

Irrigation, Yard and Pool Leaks

Irrigation line breaks and meter-box leaks are common on larger lots in Spalding County. We check them separately from the house plumbing so you know which system the water is using. Pool and spa leaks are a different specialty, so call and describe the setup and we will tell you honestly what we can do.

Official Sources for Leak and Meter Questions

Meter and billing questions belong to the water utility, so call City of Griffin customer service for your account. Repairs on your side of the meter are covered by the plumbing code, and the standard is the IPC with Georgia amendments from the Georgia Department of Community Affairs.

Georgia 811 marks buried utility lines before any digging, and a licensed plumber arranges the call for excavation work.
