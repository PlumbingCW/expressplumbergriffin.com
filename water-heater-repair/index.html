When the water heater leaks, floods the garage floor or stops making hot water, call the emergency line. We answer 24/7, and while you wait you can switch off the power or gas supply to the heater, close the cold water inlet valve on top of the tank and keep everyone away from standing water near electrical outlets.

Water heater repair is the diagnosis and fix of one failed part, such as a heating element, thermostat, gas valve, thermocouple, anode rod or relief valve, so a working tank keeps running. It is the right call when the tank itself is sound, and when it is not, we say so plainly.

When No Hot Water Is an Emergency

A heater that goes cold is an inconvenience. A heater that is leaking, smells of gas or trips a breaker over and over is an emergency, and you should treat those three differently.

  • Water on the floor: shut the cold inlet valve, turn off the power or gas, and call. Do not touch the tank if water is near the wiring.
  • Gas smell: leave the house at once and call Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321 or 911 from outside. A plumber comes after the utility clears the building.
  • No heat and no leak: check that the breaker is on or the pilot is lit, then call. Most of these are a single failed part.

After-hours calls are normal for us, and same-day repairs are offered when we have the part and the schedule allows.

Electric Heaters: Elements, Thermostats and Breakers

Most electric tanks have two heating elements, one high and one low, each controlled by a thermostat. If the water runs lukewarm and then cold, or runs out fast, one element has often burned out. If the breaker trips the moment you reset it, an element may have shorted to ground.

Element replacement is a standard repair on a healthy tank. When the tank has been in service for a decade or more, we weigh the repair against a new unit, and our water heater replacement page lays out how that decision works.

Gas Heaters: Pilots, Thermocouples and Gas Valves

A gas heater that will not stay lit usually has a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot assembly. A heater that lights but heats slowly may have sediment burying the bottom of the tank, which forces the burner to work harder and shortens the tank's life.

We do not guess on gas. If a gas valve or a vent problem is involved, we test, explain and repair it, or recommend replacement when the unit is past economical repair.

Leaks, Rust and the Anode Rod

A drip from the relief valve or a fitting is often a cheap fix. Water pooling from the bottom of the tank usually means the steel liner has failed, and no repair will cure that. Rusty hot water, popping noises and a tank older than its rated life are the signs we look for.

A sacrificial anode rod slows corrosion inside the tank, and replacing it on a younger tank can buy years. On an old tank the rod is usually spent and the damage is done. If your tank is gas fired, the same checks apply and our tankless water heater page covers the switch when you are ready.

Hybrid, Gas and Electric Models We Repair

We repair standard gas and electric storage heaters in Griffin homes and look at hybrid heat pump models case by case. Heat pump units have a compressor and a fan, and a failure there may need the manufacturer's parts and a longer lead time, so call and give us the make and model so we can answer honestly.

Standards for Water Heater Repairs

Repairs that touch gas valves, venting or electrical connections follow the same safety standards as a new install. The manufacturer's installation instructions printed with the equipment are part of the standard an inspector reads against. For a gas smell, leave the building and call Atlanta Gas Light at 877.427.4321 or 911 from outside.
